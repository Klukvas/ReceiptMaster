## 1. Clause 11 — why it needs to change

The live text caps liability at "the amount you paid to us in the twelve months before the claim".
Subscriptions are charged through Paddle as Merchant of Record, so the amount paid *to us* may be
read as zero, and a cap of zero is likely to be unenforceable or to be read against us. The draft
keeps the first paragraph (no indirect damages), replaces the cap with a floor plus the 12-month
amount, and lists what the cap does not cover.

The floor (EUR 100) is a placeholder, not a recommendation. Pick a figure with the lawyer; it
should be realistic for a service priced at the current plans.

## 2. Draft data processing terms (controller / processor)

Where this fits. The Privacy page already says that for customers' and counterparties' data the
user is the controller and we process it on their behalf (section 1, "Data of your customers and
counterparties"). What is missing is the written processor terms that a controller normally needs
from a processor. The draft below is meant to be an annex to the Terms ("Data Processing Terms"),
accepted together with them.

Roles:

- **Controller** (Ukrainian law: *володілець*): the user, for the customer and counterparty data
  they enter (names, tax IDs, IBANs, addresses, contact details).
- **Processor** (Ukrainian law: *розпорядник*): FOP Pavlenko Andrii Volodymyrovych, which stores and
  processes that data to provide the Service.
- **Sub-processors:** hosting and infrastructure providers. The list must match the table in section
  6 of the Privacy page; do not publish this annex before that table is checked.

## 3. Terminology fixes for the Russian pages (not applied)

- The Russian Privacy heading in section 2 reads "Контролер (оператор) персональных данных". "Оператор"
  is Russian-law terminology that does not match Ukrainian law, where the term is *володілець*
  (controller) and *розпорядник* (processor). Proposed heading: "Контролер (владелец) персональных
  данных", and the same word in the first sentence of that section.
- The Ukrainian Privacy page already uses "Контролер (володілець)"; no change.
- "Оператор:" is also used as a label for the service provider's contact block in
  `privacy.ru.tsx`, `privacy.uk.tsx`, `terms.ru.tsx` and `terms.uk.tsx`. There it means "the
  operator of the service", not the legal role, but next to the controller/processor wording it can be
  misread. Proposed label: "Сторона, що надає Сервіс" (uk) / "Сторона, предоставляющая Сервис" (ru), or
  simply "Виконавець" / "Исполнитель" if the lawyer prefers the contract role.

## 4. Questions for the lawyer

1. Is a cap of "greater of 12 months' fees and a fixed floor" enforceable for business users under
   Ukrainian law, and is it acceptable for consumers? Which carve-outs must be listed? What floor is
   realistic?
2. Because Paddle is the Merchant of Record, who is the contractual counterparty for the subscription
   fee, and does "fees you paid … through our payment provider" fit that structure?
3. Is a separate annex with processor terms required, or enough, under the Law of Ukraine on Personal
   Data Protection, given that users are mostly sole traders entering customer data? Does anything
   change for users in the EU (GDPR Art. 28)?
4. Do we need a written processing agreement with each sub-processor (hosting, email, payments,
   Telegram), and which of them are outside Ukraine and the EEA?
5. Notice period for breaches: "without undue delay" or a fixed number of hours?
6. Is the statement that voided and failed documents are kept as long as the account exists
   acceptable under data minimisation, or should failed documents be purged after a fixed period? (The
   product currently keeps them; an engineering change is cheap if a period is required.)
7. Does keeping security logs with masked counterparty details for the life of the account need a
   stated retention period?
8. The public document link shows seller IBAN and buyer requisites by design. Is that acceptable as is,
   or do we need an explicit warning when a link is created?
9. Terms clause 11.4 puts responsibility for the correctness and legal use of documents on the user.
   Is the wording sufficient for the invoice and act (not primary documents), the non-fiscal sales
   receipt and the packing list, or should each document type carry a one-line notice on the PDF?
10. VAT: the documents print 20% for VAT payers as a fixed assumption. Should this be reviewed by an
    accountant before it is offered to VAT payers?
